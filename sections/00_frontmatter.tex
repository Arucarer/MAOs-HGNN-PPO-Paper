\begin{abstract}
Reactive scheduling in flexible job shops with multi-level assembly operations requires production states that retain technological precedence, resource competition, and assembly dependencies. Processing-time variations, machine breakdowns, and urgent order arrivals can propagate through these relations, affecting both makespan and deviation from a baseline schedule. This study investigates dynamic heterogeneous graph state encoding within an HGNN-PPO rule-selection framework. Typed relation aggregation distinguishes production entities and dependencies, while six meta-path patterns represent complementary processing--assembly semantics. Semantic attention and residual fusion combine these representations, and Role-aware Mean Pooling Fusion supplies routing, sequencing, and recovery states. PPO selects a dispatching rule for the active decision type; the environment decodes the rule into a feasible scheduling decision. Available comparative results are reported for the supplied mixed rule pool. An offline-screened pool of simple and manually designed rules is a planned configuration requiring separate implementation and training. Controlled encoder comparisons and verified disturbance coverage are needed to establish the scope of the representation benefit.
\end{abstract}
